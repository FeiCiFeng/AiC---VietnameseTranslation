Strawberry Tart
Crispy golden tart with strawberries and honey

Ingredients: Flour, butter, sugar, water, salt, honey, lemon juice, strawberries, red currant jam

Preheat oven to 190 (375). Mix flour, butter, sugar, and salt in bowl until crumbly. Add water in small amounts until clumps form. Roll clumps into ball and press into tart pan. Poke holes in bottom of dough and bake for 15 minutes or until golden brown. Mix sugar, lemon juice, and red currant jam in small saucepan. Place saucepan on stove and bring to low boil. Pour the hot mixture into the tart and place fresh strawberries evenly throughout. Drizzle honey evenly onto strawberries. Try with cold whipped cream for an extra sweet treat.
*
Chicken Soup w/ Soy Milk

Ingredients: Cabbage, chicken breast, leek, ginger, soy milk, water

Grind chicken meat and roll into tablespoon sized balls. When water comes to a boil, carefully add the chicken meat. Skim any bits off the top and sides. Simmer heat and add chopped cabbage and leek. Remove from heat and add soy milk. Season to taste and reheat for 3 minutes, then serve. Cabbage can be substituted with turnips for a more peppery flavor.
*
Lamb's Meat Breaded Meatballs

Ingredients: Lamb, cabbage, onion, bread crumbs, egg, salt, pepper, licorice powder

Finely chop half a head of cabbage and sprinkle with salt. Mix cabbage with ground lamb, onion, cabbage, egg, licorice powder, salt, and pepper in a bowl and knead into small balls. Heat oil in a frying pan and sear the meatballs until the outside is brown. Let cool slightly, then add bread crumbs to bowl and roll meatballs to form a shell. Brush shells with beaten egg and poke holes before baking. Cook in oven until golden brown then serve.

The Brain

The command center of your nervous system. The brain is a specialized organ housed in your skull that is responsible for receiving and processing information from the sensory nervous system.

The cerebrum controls logical thinking and language, the cerebellum coordinates movement, and the brainstem is responsible for automating breathing, heartbeat, and other vital functions.

Everything that you do, think, and experience is a result of your brain reacting to the data it takes in.
*
Stomach

A muscular, hollow organ that plays a vital role in the digestion of food.

Enzymes and acids are secreted by gastric glands which break down and soften food before sending it to the small intestine. 
*
Small and large intestine

The small intestine is where the majority of nutrients from food are absorbed into the body. It has three distinct regions - the duodenum, jejunum, and ileum.

The large intestine is where any remaining liquids are absorbed into the body and where waste matter begins to accumulate. There are also bacteria within the intestine which help with vitamins and immunity.
*
Liver and pancreas

The liver is the largest organ inside your body and performs a variety of functions, including metabolizing nutrients, breaking down toxins, and the decomposition of red blood cells. Actihol and other drugs are also processed here.

The pancreas works with the small intestine to break down fats, sugars, and proteins. It also helps regulate blood sugar levels.
*
Kidneys

A pair of bean-shaped organs located on both sides of your waist. Kidneys help filter waste from the body and send it to the bladder to be excreted. They also play a key role in red blood cell production and regulate your body’s water and electrolyte levels.
*
Dilators

A pair of small organs found on both sides of your chest. A subset of the respiratory system, these organs take mana from the air you breathe and store it in red blood cells to circulate throughout your body.

Mana can still be drawn out once metabolized, but may be painful depending on the method.
*
Heart

Part of the circulatory system. Pumps blood throughout your body to get oxygen and mana to organs that need it. Has four chambers and works by expanding and contracting based on electrical signals.
